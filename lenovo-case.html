Lenovo e-commerce redesign

UX concept for the Lenovo online store: an end-to-end laptop purchase journey, from the first visit to checkout and post-purchase support.

Figma file ↗

Product problem

The core problem is how hard it is to choose a device. Users face a large number of models and technical specs, which raises cognitive load and slows down the decision.

For the business, this means

  • lower conversion to purchase
  • longer device selection time
  • high drop-off at the model selection stage

Design goal: reduce selection complexity and shorten the path to purchase.

Scenario design

I designed an end-to-end user scenario for purchasing a laptop.

Why choosing a laptop is hard

Buying a laptop is one of the most complex scenarios in e-commerce. Users face several barriers at once:

  • a large number of models
  • complex technical specifications
  • multiple configurations of the same device
  • hard-to-navigate catalog

Key insights

Users often don't understand the differences between product lines. The number of specs raises cognitive load. Comparing models takes a lot of time, and configuring a device feels complicated and unclear.

As a result, users

  • postpone the decision
  • spend a long time studying specs
  • leave without purchasing

Product strategy

The core UX strategy is reducing cognitive load and simplifying device selection. Four principles support it.

1. Selection in stages

Selection is split into two steps: first a product line, then a specific model. Users narrow options gradually instead of facing the whole catalog at once.

2. Better in-category navigation

A category landing page explains the differences between laptop series and helps pick the right segment.

3. Catalog as a comparison tool

The catalog is designed as a decision-making tool, not just a product list. Cards show key specs so models can be compared faster.

4. Personalization via configurator

Users adjust specs to their needs and see the updated price instantly.

Process & outcome

System approach

I designed for scalability, so the interface can extend to other product categories:

  • reusable UI components
  • consistent product card structure
  • unified filter system
  • consistent configuration logic

Team collaboration

I worked with the art director and the development team. Together we:

  • discussed the catalog's product structure
  • aligned on user scenarios
  • accounted for technical constraints
  • validated the configurator's feasibility

After the interfaces were designed, I took part in discussions on implementing the key scenarios.

Results

A UX concept for the Lenovo e-commerce platform covering the full device purchase journey. It was used in a tender to demonstrate the UX approach to a tech online store.

What I'd improve after launch

1. Device comparison

Compare several models right in the catalog. Expected: faster decisions, more click-through to product pages.

2. Personal device finder

An interactive laptop finder via a short quiz. Expected: less selection time, more click-through to product pages.

3. Richer product page

Use-case scenarios, visual explanation of specs, similar model recommendations.

4. Checkout A/B tests

Fewer steps, simpler forms, auto-fill. Expected: lower abandonment, higher order completion.
